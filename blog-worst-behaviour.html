Why Your Child Saves Their Worst Behaviour for You

2 min read

If your child holds it together all day at school and then falls completely apart the moment they get home — you are not doing something wrong.

You are doing something right.

Children fall apart in front of the person they trust most not to leave. Your child is not targeting you. They are safe with you. You are the place where they can finally stop holding it together.

All day long they are managing. Following rules. Sitting still. Navigating friendships. Keeping their emotions in check. That takes enormous energy for a small person.

By the time they walk through your door, they are running on empty — and you are their safe place to land.

That does not make it easy to live with. The meltdowns, the backtalk, the sudden tears over nothing — these are hard. But understanding why it happens changes how you respond.

When you know your child is not giving you a hard time but having a hard time, the question shifts. Instead of "why are they doing this to me" you start asking "what do they need right now?"

Sometimes the answer is food. Sometimes it is quiet. Sometimes it is just you, nearby, without asking anything of them.

Try offering a soft landing before anything else. Snack first. Questions later. Your presence without pressure is often the fastest way through.

You are their safe place. That is not a burden. That is the whole point.
