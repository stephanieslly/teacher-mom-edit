What Teachers Know About Transitions (That Most Parents Don't)

3 min read

Transitions are one of the hardest parts of a child's day — and most parents don't realize it because transitions look so ordinary.

Moving from play to dinner. From screen time to bedtime. From home to school. From one activity to the next. These moments require a child to stop what they are doing, shift their attention, and start something new — all while managing whatever feelings come with that change.

Teachers know this. It is why classrooms have transition warnings, cleanup songs, visual timers, and consistent routines around every shift in the day.

At home, transitions often happen without warning. "Turn it off now." "We have to go." "Stop what you are doing." And then we are surprised when kids resist, melt down, or move at a pace that feels impossible.

The fix is not complicated. It is a warning. "Five more minutes and then we are leaving." "Two more minutes of play and then it is time for dinner." That small heads-up gives a child's brain time to shift. It does not always prevent every protest — but it dramatically reduces the friction.

Another teacher trick is naming what comes next. Not just ending one thing, but bridging to the next. "We are finishing up here and then we get to do bath time together." The transition becomes a bridge instead of a wall.

Try adding one transition warning to the hardest moment of your day this week. Just one. Notice what changes.
